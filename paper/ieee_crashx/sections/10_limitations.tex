\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Test-set size.}
One hundred and fifty videos is enough to detect the adaptation effects ($p<10^{-4}$ on every completeness metric) and to rule out large \tcd{} effects, but not to detect small ones: the paired interval on NLI-Score at $\alpha{=}0.5$ spans $[-0.034,+0.156]$.
A \tcd{} gain of $+0.05$ NLI-Score or $-0.01$ \Hcost{} would require roughly four times the data to establish.
The no-crash probe uses six videos; we report counts, not rates, and draw no inference beyond ``fabrication occurs and worsens under contrast''.

\paragraph{Automatic judges.}
The structured costs depend on regular-expression field extraction and token-overlap matching, which we chose for determinism.
They are lenient on vehicle \emph{type} (any shared token counts) and strict on colour; we report both.
The NLI cross-encoder abstains on most pairs and correlates weakly with the structured cost (Spearman $-0.10$ with \Hcost{}).
An LLM judge in the ARGUS style~\cite{argus2025} would give sentence-level hallucination and omission rates on the free text itself; we did not use one because we wanted every number reproducible offline, and because judge-model choice would add a second source of variance to a study whose main point is variance.

\paragraph{No human evaluation.}
We prepared a blinded 200-item, three-annotator Likert protocol (temporal accuracy, faithfulness, explanation quality) and release the item pack, but the annotations were not completed at the scale the protocol requires.
The structured costs are therefore the paper's faithfulness ground truth.
Human ratings would most likely sharpen, not reverse, the omission/hallucination asymmetry, since the fabricated collisions in Section~\ref{sec:nocrash} are unambiguous to a human viewer.

\paragraph{Frame budget and time information.}
Eight frames at $\leq224$\,px and no absolute time stamps is the standard, cheap usage of these models and the setting our compute allowed, but it is also the reason the temporal prior arises.
We have shown that the prior exists and beats the model; we have \emph{not} shown that a time-aware input~\cite{timechat,vtimellm,lita}, denser sampling, or a time-token objective would remove it on CCD.
That is the obvious next experiment.

\paragraph{Prompt presupposition.}
The prompt asks the model to ``analyze this car crash video''.
For the 5.5\% of clips that are near-misses this presupposes a false fact.
We kept the prompt identical for all systems because changing it would confound the comparison, but the fabrication counts in Table~\ref{tab:nocrash} are an upper bound on what a neutrally phrased prompt would produce.

\paragraph{Validation split and model selection.}
The 150-video validation split was reserved but not used for checkpoint selection; the final checkpoint is the last of five epochs.
No hyperparameter of \crashlogic{} or \tcd{} was tuned on the test split---$\alpha{=}0.5$ was chosen before the paired tests were run, and the paired tests then declined to support it.

\paragraph{Single backbone, single seed.}
All adapted systems derive from one QLoRA run of one 7B backbone.
Variance across training seeds is unmeasured, and stronger or time-aware backbones may not share the temporal prior.
The zero-shot comparison spans four models from two families and the hallucination cost is high for all of them, which suggests the omission/hallucination asymmetry is not specific to Qwen2.5-VL, but we have adapted only one.

\paragraph{No proprietary systems.}
GPT-4o, Gemini, and similar API models are absent.
VRU-Accident~\cite{vruaccident} reports that Gemini-1.5-Flash leads its dense-captioning leaderboard, and ARGUS~\cite{argus2025} reports that Gemini-2.0-Flash still hallucinates in about 41\% of caption sentences; we expect proprietary systems to reduce omission substantially and hallucination less, but that is a prediction, not a result.

\paragraph{Simplified SEASON.}
Our temporal homogenisation acts in pixel space rather than on vision-encoder features, and our frame-attention estimate is a uniform partition of the key sequence.
The full-SEASON row is therefore a faithful \emph{implementation of the recipe} on this backbone, not a reproduction of the original system.
