\section{Task, Data, and Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:dataset}

\subsection{Task}
Given a dashcam clip $V$, a system must produce a free-text \textbf{Explanation} of the collision and, optionally, structured forensic fields.
We evaluate the Explanation as the primary object and use the structured fields only to \emph{audit} it: to decide which of the model's claims are missing (omission) or contradicted (hallucination) relative to ground truth.

\subsection{\dataset{}: our human-annotated crash explanation dataset}
\label{sec:ourdata}

Existing dashcam accident corpora were built for anticipation, detection, or multiple-choice question answering, and none of them provides the dense, field-aligned forensic narrative that an explanation model must be scored against.
We therefore \textbf{constructed our own annotation resource}, \dataset{}, and release it with this article as
\code{Car\_Crash\_Text\_Dataset\_ground\_truth.xlsx}.

\paragraph{Video source and annotation contribution.}
The underlying visual material is 1{,}500 five-second dashcam clips at 10\,fps drawn from the publicly available Car Crash Dataset (CCD)~\cite{bao2020nondeterministic}, which distributes videos with anticipation-oriented labels only.
\emph{Every textual and forensic annotation used in this article is original work produced by our annotation team}; we do not reuse any caption, explanation, severity grade, impact description, or crash-window label from prior releases.
\dataset{} contributes 1{,}500 human-written multi-sentence explanations (143{,}968 words in total) together with a nine-field structured schema, and it is the first resource on these clips that supports separating omission from hallucination at field level.

\paragraph{Annotation schema.}
Each of the 1{,}500 rows is annotated by a human annotator who watched the clip and filled the following fields:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \code{Severity of the Crash} --- \code{minor} / \code{moderate} / \code{severe} / \code{fatal} (clips with no collision receive no severity and are treated as \code{n/a}).
  \item \code{Type of Vehicles involved} --- colour-and-type strings for every agent, e.g.\ ``yellow car, black car'' (647 distinct strings).
  \item \code{No. of Vehicles involved} --- integer count; $0$ marks a near-miss with no collision.
  \item \code{Location of impact} --- free-text contact geometry per agent, e.g.\ ``front-right corner of yellow car, front-left corner of black car'' (1{,}335 distinct strings).
  \item \code{Start of Crash} / \code{End of Crash} --- the crash window in \code{hh:mm:ss}, giving an integer-second interval $[t_s^\star,t_e^\star]$.
  \item \code{Explanation} --- a multi-sentence causal narrative ($96.0\pm25.5$ words, $5.0$ sentences on average) that names the agents, the manoeuvre, the cause, and the contact point.
  \item \code{Weather Conditions} --- \code{normal} / \code{snowy} / \code{rainy} / \code{night} / \code{mist} / \code{cloudy} and combinations.
  \item \code{Camera View} --- the ego vehicle and, where relevant, the vehicle it follows (178 distinct descriptions).
  \item \code{Ambiguity} --- an explicit note when the annotator judged some evidence uncertain (103 clips), e.g.\ ``unclear traffic signals'', ``collision not clear''.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Annotation protocol.}
Annotators viewed each clip frame-by-frame at 10\,fps and were instructed to (i) describe only what is visible, (ii) name agents by colour and type rather than by role alone, (iii) mark the crash window at the first and last frame in which contact is visible, and (iv) record any residual uncertainty in the \code{Ambiguity} field instead of guessing.
The \code{Ambiguity} column is the reason we can quantify judgement calls rather than silently absorbing them into the labels.
Two rows contain unparseable timestamps and are dropped, leaving 1{,}498 annotated clips.

\paragraph{Label distribution.}
Severity is dominated by \code{moderate} (692), followed by \code{minor} (379), \code{severe} (301), and \code{fatal} (43); this $46\%$ majority class is the constant-predictor baseline we compare against in Section~\ref{sec:fields}.
Most crashes involve two vehicles (1{,}228 clips), with 113 three-vehicle and 12 four- or five-vehicle events.
Weather is \code{normal} in 941 clips, \code{snowy} in 410, and \code{rainy} in 122.

\paragraph{Split protocol.}
We draw a stratified 80/10/10 split by severity (\code{StratifiedShuffleSplit}, seed 42): 1{,}198 / 150 / 150 videos (66 / 8 / 9 no-crash; Table~\ref{tab:dataset}).
Video IDs are pairwise disjoint across splits; severity proportions match to within $0.3$ points.
The validation split was reserved but not used for model selection in the final run (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\paragraph{No-crash clips.}
Eighty-three clips are near-misses: our annotators recorded zero involved vehicles and wrote an explanation stating that no collision occurred (66 in train, 8 in validation, 9 in test).
Six of the nine test explanations say so unambiguously (``fortunately, no accident occurred''); three describe an evasive manoeuvre without stating the outcome.
We use the six unambiguous clips as a small but decisive probe for fabricated collisions (Section~\ref{sec:nocrash}).
Their ground-truth window is empty, so they contribute tIoU $=0$ to all systems.

\paragraph{Temporal structure of the ground truth.}
Crash windows are short and concentrated: on the 141 crash videos of the test split the window starts in $[2,4)$\,s for 80\% of clips and lasts $1.55$\,s on average (77 windows of 1\,s, 39 of 2\,s, 17 of 3\,s).
The most frequent training window is $[3,4]$\,s (314 of 1{,}132 crash clips), followed by $[2,3]$\,s (253).
This concentration is a property of real dashcam collisions rather than an annotation artefact, and it is exactly what makes a prior-only baseline necessary (Section~\ref{sec:metrics-temporal}).

\subsection{Metrics}
\label{sec:metrics}

\paragraph{Lexical quality.}
On the Explanation field we report corpus-level BLEU-4~\cite{papineni2002bleu} via sacreBLEU~\cite{post2018sacrebleu}, and mean per-video ROUGE-L~\cite{lin2004rouge}, METEOR~\cite{banerjee2005meteor}, CIDEr~\cite{vedantam2015cider}, and BERTScore-F1~\cite{zhang2020bertscore} (\code{bert-base-uncased}).
Because corpus BLEU and mean per-video BLEU differ ($0.142$ vs.\ $0.123$ for \crashlogic), we use corpus BLEU in headline tables and per-video BLEU only inside paired tests.
For systems that do not emit an \code{Explanation:} field, the entire output is scored as the explanation.

\paragraph{Entailment (NLI).}
A cross-encoder NLI model (\code{cross-encoder/nli-deberta-v3-small}~\cite{he2023debertav3}) scores each pair with the ground-truth forensic text as \emph{premise} and the model output as \emph{hypothesis}.
The premise concatenates the structured fields rendered as sentences with the reference Explanation.
Let $P_e$, $P_c$, $P_n$ be entailment, contradiction, and neutral probabilities.
We report
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{NLI\text{-}Score}=P_e-P_c,\qquad \mathrm{NLI\text{-}Loss}=-\log P_e ,
\label{eq:nli}
\end{equation}
both with the Explanation as hypothesis (\emph{NLI}) and with the \emph{entire} output as hypothesis (\emph{Full-NLI}).
The second variant is immune to how the Explanation is extracted and therefore fairer to zero-shot systems that ignore the output format.

\paragraph{Omission and hallucination costs.}
\label{sec:metrics-ho}
Following ARGUS~\cite{argus2025}, we define two normalised costs per video, but over \dataset{}'s five structured fields $\mathcal{F}=\{\text{severity},\text{impact},\text{vehicles},\text{weather},\text{window}\}$ plus the Explanation, so that they are deterministic and reproducible without an LLM judge.
Let $A\subseteq\mathcal{F}$ be the fields the model \emph{asserts} (parsed from its output, with keyword fallbacks for unstructured text) and $G\subseteq\mathcal{F}\cup\{\text{expl}\}$ the fields the ground truth specifies.
A field $f\in A$ \emph{conflicts} if the asserted value is disjoint from the ground truth (severity: label mismatch; impact/vehicles/weather: no shared token; window: tIoU $<0.1$).
A field $f\in G$ is \emph{omitted} if it is not asserted or its value shares nothing with the ground truth (window: tIoU $<0.25$; Explanation: fewer than 8 words).
Then
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{H}}=\frac{|\{f\in A:\ f\ \text{conflicts}\}|}{|A|},\qquad
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{O}}=\frac{|\{f\in G:\ f\ \text{omitted}\}|}{|G|},
\label{eq:ho}
\end{equation}
with $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{H}}{=}1$ if $A{=}\emptyset$.
\Hcost{} is a \emph{conditional} rate---the fraction of what the model chose to say that is wrong---so it is comparable across systems that assert different numbers of fields, and we always report the assertion rate $|A|/|\mathcal{F}|$ beside it.
We also report per-field conflict rates (fraction of all videos with a conflict on field $f$), and a stricter \emph{colour-disjoint} agent check that flags an output whose vehicle colours share nothing with the ground truth, because token overlap on generic types (``car'') makes the field-level vehicle check lenient.
The colour-and-type convention in our \code{Type of Vehicles involved} field is what makes this strict check possible.

\paragraph{Temporal grounding, with prior baselines.}
\label{sec:metrics-temporal}
For predicted window $[\hat t_s,\hat t_e]$ and reference $[t_s^\star,t_e^\star]$,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{tIoU}=\frac{|[\hat t_s,\hat t_e]\cap[t_s^\star,t_e^\star]|}{|[\hat t_s,\hat t_e]\cup[t_s^\star,t_e^\star]|},
\label{eq:tiou}
\end{equation}
averaged over all 150 videos (missing or unparseable windows score 0), plus hit rates THR@0.25 and THR@0.50.
To interpret tIoU we add three \emph{prior-only baselines} that ignore the video: the constant windows $[2,3]$\,s, $[3,4]$\,s (the training mode), and $[2,4]$\,s.
We also report the Spearman correlation between predicted and true start times over crash videos and the multiset of distinct windows a system emits.
A system whose tIoU does not exceed the constant baselines, or whose predicted starts are uncorrelated with true starts, has not localised anything.

\paragraph{Error taxonomy.}
\label{sec:taxonomy}
We code each output on crash videos with five non-exclusive failure labels plus \textsc{Clean}, using automatic proxies so that counts are reproducible:
\textsc{No-Narrative} (under 8 words, or under 30 words with no collision verb);
\textsc{Fluent-Fabrication} ($\geq$30 words, no collision verb);
\textsc{Wrong-Agent} (colour-disjoint vehicles);
\textsc{Wrong-Time} (window asserted with tIoU $<0.1$);
\textsc{Wrong-Cause} (agents consistent but NLI contradiction $P_c>0.5$);
\textsc{Under-Specific} (ground truth names a colour, output names none).
The proxies are conservative for \textsc{Wrong-Cause}, which we therefore read as a lower bound.

\subsection{Statistics}
\label{sec:stats}
All systems are evaluated on the same 150 videos, so every comparison is paired.
For each metric we report the mean, a 95\% percentile bootstrap interval (2{,}000 resamples)~\cite{efron1993bootstrap}, and for each pair of systems the mean per-video difference with a paired-bootstrap interval (5{,}000 resamples), a two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank $p$-value~\cite{wilcoxon1945,dror2018hitchhiker}, and win/loss/tie counts.
Corpus BLEU has no per-video form; its paired test uses per-video BLEU.
We use $p<0.05$ and call an effect \emph{directional} when the paired interval includes zero.
